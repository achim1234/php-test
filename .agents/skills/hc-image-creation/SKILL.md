# Industrial Couture

Create cinematic fashion photographs where unconventional materials
become wearable architectural sculptures.

Follow the user's latest instructions over these defaults.
Keep locations, materials and creative direction flexible.

## Required deliverables

For each post, create:
- Five individual images.
- One overview assembled from those five final images.
- One text file containing a title, teaser and exactly five hashtags.

Default to square 1:1 images, including the overview.
Use a different format or quantity when explicitly requested.

Save the complete post under:

public/posts/post_{timestamp}/ (relative to the project root)

## Visual direction

- Favor photorealistic editorial photography.
- Combine dramatic silhouettes with believable material textures.
- Keep the garment the main subject.
- Show complete garments, including shoulders, hem and train.
- Include footwear or mannequin bases where applicable.
- Leave adequate margins and enough space to understand the setting.
- Use natural skin texture and anatomically plausible poses.
- Avoid text, logos and watermarks unless requested.
- Use atmosphere and dramatic lighting deliberately rather than
  applying the same effects to every image.

## Plan the five images

Choose five complementary images that suit the user's brief.
Avoid producing five nearly identical frames.

For a garment editorial, a useful sequence is:
1. Full front view establishing the garment.
2. Three-quarter view showing depth and construction.
3. Side view showing the silhouette.
4. Rear view showing the back construction.
5. A second pose or setting that completes the story.

For a transformation story, use five progressive stages.

Adapt the plan when the user requests particular views, locations,
subjects or stages. Keep identities and garment design consistent.

Use detail views only when requested or when they materially improve
the post. Otherwise keep the complete garment visible.

## Material direction

Use the requested materials.
Default to aluminium only when no material has been established.

### Aluminium

Specify the intended manufacturing appearance:
- Brushed sheet metal with directional grain.
- Polished edges and controlled reflections.
- Cast aluminium with subtle surface irregularities.
- Folded panels with believable thickness.
- Rivets or seams where appropriate to the design.

Make aluminium visibly metallic rather than silver fabric.
Use crumpled foil only when requested or present in the reference.

Keep the model's skin natural unless metallic skin is requested.

### Molded plastic

Use rigid thermoformed shells, curved panels, glossy surfaces,
translucency, refraction and visible edges.

Distinguish plastic from aluminium through its optical properties.
Use opaque lining where needed for the intended garment coverage.

### Casting sand

Show compacted granular brown or ochre material:
- Thick brittle crust.
- Jagged fracture edges.
- Loose grains and falling chunks.
- Dust and debris.

Clearly distinguish the external sand mold from the metal beneath.

### Other materials

Adapt the treatment to the requested material.
Consider texture, weight, stiffness, transparency, construction
and interaction with light.

Preserve mixed materials when extracting an existing garment.
Do not replace cloth, colored panels or decorative elements with
metal unless requested.

## Garment design vocabulary

Choose a coherent silhouette rather than combining every feature.

Possible directions:
- Faceted corset with angular asymmetric skirt panels.
- One-shoulder ribbon bodice with broad curved metal petals.
- Smooth dome-shaped bell skirt.
- Tall side flanges rising from the skirt to frame the torso.
- Exaggerated upward-pointing shoulders.
- Architectural hip structures and an open-front skirt.
- Sculptural molded shells and dramatic trains.

For a new variant, change silhouette and construction meaningfully.
For an existing design, preserve its identifying features.

Treat these examples as starting points, not a fixed collection.

## Presentation modes

### Human model

Use the selected adult model consistently.
Choose deliberate editorial poses or a clear walking stride.
Keep the garment physically integrated with the body.

If no identity reference exists, create a model appropriate to the
brief without claiming continuity with an unseen person.

### Tailor's mannequin

Use a traditional dressmaking form:
- Linen-covered torso with tailoring seams.
- Headless and armless construction.
- Short neck stump with wooden cap or finial.
- Central metal pole and grounded base.

Remove human hair, skin, hands, legs and footwear.
Show the torso or support pole through garment openings.
Do not substitute a faceless human for a dress form.

Adapt mannequin color and finish when requested.

### Alternate views

Explicitly specify front, side, three-quarter or rear view.
For a rear view, show the back of the head and garment.
Infer unseen construction conservatively and consistently.

## Settings and lighting

Use the user's requested setting.
Retain the established setting for follow-up edits unless changed.

If no setting is specified or established, choose one that supports
the garment and briefly state the creative choice.

Possible settings:
- Black studio with focused sculptural lighting.
- Industrial foundry with steel structures and dusty light shafts.
- Contemporary city center with glass towers.
- Luxury shopping district with refined storefronts and terraces.
- Weathered streets with brick, shutters, graffiti and utility wires.
- Foggy meadow, farmland or mountain landscape.
- Gallery, workshop, runway or architectural interior.

Do not default to a particular city or country.
Do not add regional landmarks, transport or cultural symbols
unless requested or relevant to the established scene.

Interpret "shady" as moody and weathered without automatically
adding violence or criminal stereotypes.

Match reflections, shadows and color temperature to the setting.

Research visual references when the user requests research or when
specific real-world architecture or infrastructure must be accurate.
Prefer authoritative local sources where available.

Separate observed features from invented scenery.
Do not claim a fictional composite depicts an exact real location.

## Casting and demolding sequences

Treat foundry scenes as surreal fashion imagery, not manufacturing
instructions.

Maintain the same models, underlying garment design, camera and
lighting throughout the sequence.

Use these five stages when appropriate:

1. Mold:
   Show the garment enclosed in a thick compacted sand shell,
   with little or no metal exposed.

2. First opening:
   Show the first broken sections of the mold revealing solid metal.

3. Partial release:
   Expose substantial metal while retaining large sand-shell sections.

4. Nearly released:
   Leave only a few patches of sand crust at the hips or lower skirt.

5. Final reveal:
   Remove all sand crust from the garment.
   Leave broken mold debris on the floor.
   Present the complete finished metal design.

If requested, include a second adult model in a contrasting
sculptural garment gently chipping the external sand crust
with a small hammer.

Keep this second model and outfit consistent.
In the final frame, lower the hammer and clearly show that
the work is complete.

Use cooled, solid metal for demolding.
Direct hammer contact at the external mold.
Do not depict injury or molten metal contacting a person's skin.

For a pouring scene, place molten metal in a separate mold or
background foundry apparatus.

Adapt the stages to explicit user requests.

11. Write post_text.txt.
12. Verify the complete post and provide concise delivery links.

needed to limit identity or garment drift.

Correct concrete problems with targeted edits.
Avoid unnecessary regeneration after the requirements are satisfied.

Follow supported reference-image and export mechanisms.

## Prompt structure

Build image prompts in this order:

- Output: aspect ratio and photographic style.
- References: identity, garment, setting or preceding stage.
- Subject: human model or tailor's mannequin.
- Garment: silhouette, construction and materials.
- Action: pose, walking or transformation stage.
- Setting: environment and relevant details.
- Lighting: mood and material reflections.
- Composition: view, scale and garment visibility.
- Invariants: features that must remain unchanged.
- Exclusions: only those needed for the request.

## Overview image

Create post_0 only after post_1 through post_5 are finalized.

Build it directly from those five saved images using an available
image-compositing tool. Do not ask the image generator to recreate
the images as a collage.

Default to a square layout:
- Place post_1 in a larger panel occupying the left half.
- Arrange post_2 through post_5 in a 2-by-2 grid on the right.
- Fit each complete source image within its panel.
- Preserve aspect ratios without stretching.
- Use a black or neutral background and consistent gutters.
- Include each image exactly once.
- Do not add titles, labels or decorative elements unless requested.

Use padding instead of cropping important garment details.
Use sufficient resolution to keep the smaller images readable;
prefer at least 2048 by 2048 pixels when practical.

Save the overview as post_0.png or post_0.jpg according to its
actual encoding.

## Post text

Always save a UTF-8 plain-text file named post_text.txt.

Write one title, one teaser and exactly five relevant hashtags
for the complete five-image post.

Use this format:

Title: {short title}

Teaser: {one or two concise sentences}

Hashtags: #TagOne #TagTwo #TagThree #TagFour #TagFive

### Writing direction

- Write post_text.txt in English by default.
- Use another language only when the user explicitly requests it for the post.
- Keep the title short and memorable.
- Emphasize material, silhouette, atmosphere or transformation.
- Describe AI-created concepts honestly.
- Avoid claims of real fabrication for generated fashion concepts.
- Avoid generic promotional filler and promises of engagement.
- Use exactly five distinct, relevant hashtags.
- Include geography only when relevant to the brief.
- Do not add a city or country by default.

Create one caption for the complete post unless separate captions
or multiple options are explicitly requested.
